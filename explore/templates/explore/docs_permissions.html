Who may write what

HWDB decides every write; the Explorer only warns before one it expects to be refused. An account has three things that matter: it is active (everyone who can sign in), it may hold the administrator and architect flags, and it holds zero or more roles. A component type lists the roles that may write to it. The training site introduces the flags; the rules below are what the database applies.

Roles are per instance: the development and production databases keep separate accounts, roles and flags. Holding the architect flag on dev says nothing about prod.

What the Explorer gates itself

On top of HWDB’s own checks, some Explorer pages open only for certain accounts, so that a type-level change is not made by accident. These are the Explorer’s rules, not HWDB’s:

PageOpens for
Checklist editor, ES config, New item, Edit items, Upload sheetanyone on a write instance; HWDB applies the type’s roles on save
Positions pageadministrator or architect; editing needs administrator, cloning the type needs architect
Category dropdown on a type page (shipping container, consortium type)architect
Production plan and Component list formsone of the type’s roles, as HWDB applies them; the page says which roles when you hold none
Checklists that name their own rolesas the schema says; the fill page names the roles up front

Seeing what you hold

  • The pages that write to a type show a warning naming the type’s roles and yours when you hold none of them, and say so when the type lists none at all.
  • Your roles and flags are read from HWDB once per session. After a grant, sign out and in again.
  • The type’s roles are on its page in the HWDB web UI; ask a consortium liaison with the administrator flag to add you to one.
